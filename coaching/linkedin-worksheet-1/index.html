Let's rework your LinkedIn.

This actionable is about rebuilding your LinkedIn profile around your strengths and competencies, so it reflects what you bring rather than where you are.

Part 1

The kind of work you're aiming for

This tells me what to aim the rest of your profile at. Tick anything that interests you, even if you haven't decided.

Part 2

Your organizing work

This isn't on your profile yet, and it's your strongest experience. Volunteer work belongs in the Experience section exactly like a paid role: same title, same dates, same bullet points. If you've held more than one, answer for the most substantial.

Use the title that matches the work you did. If you handled the finances, you were a treasurer. If you ran the accounts, you were a communications lead.

If you did it and could explain it to someone, tick it.

Pick one from the list above and write a line on what you were responsible for. Ticking a box shows you did it once; this shows it was yours, and it becomes the first bullet point in the entry.

A document, a process, an account, a system. "It's just a spreadsheet" is how people talk themselves out of the best line on their profile.

Write what you were asked to take on, who asked, and roughly when. If someone chose to hand you more, that says something about you that you couldn't credibly say about yourself.

Part 3

Your two main roles

Both of these are on your profile with almost nothing written under them. One sentence per box is enough to fix that.

How to write these: start with a verb, say what you did, then the scale. "Helped out with the program" becomes "Ran weekly activity sessions for groups of 20 children." Same work, stated clearly.

Activity Leader, YMCA Canada, March 2026 to present

Manager, Pedalheads, March 2026

You already have a description here. What's missing is the team: how many people, doing what, and what you decided rather than carried out. That is what makes the title mean something.

Part 4

Your two shorter roles

These were brief, so one line each is enough.

What did you do, and for how many people?

First say what the role actually covered: painting only, or also knocking on doors, quoting jobs, or dealing with customers. Then add the numbers for whichever it was: houses painted, doors knocked, clients handled.

Part 5

How you work with people

This becomes the description at the top of your profile, which you don't have yet.

Two or three sentences. This usually gets a better answer than "what are your strengths", because you're measuring against something real.

Part 6

Your three programs

What does this give you that you intend to use? Language, a region, writing, cultural work?

Part 7

Your skills list

The five on your profile describe roles you've held, not the work you're aiming at. Tick the ones you could give a real example of, and leave the rest blank: you'd have to defend them in an interview.

Part 8
